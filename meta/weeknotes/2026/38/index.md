# 2026 - Week 38

My, how time flies. Another two weeks ticked off our working lifespans with not much to show for our efforts. Please be assured, we've not been slacking on the taxpayers' dime. There's just a lot of plates spinning and nothing quite making it to completion. Some jottings on where we're at and what we've been trying to do.

Efforts to upgrade our thesaurus management service remain blocked as the vendor attempts to convince their computers that [adding one to a number cannot be beyond the wit of man](https://ukparliament.github.io/ontologies/meta/weeknotes/2026/34/#everyone-has-a-plan). Some of which have been hanging around for the best part of a decade.

It's all been a little quiet in [procedural map making](https://ukparliament.github.io/ontologies/procedure/maps/) land of late, but not this week. The recent arrival of the [Business, Innovation, Science and Trade Committee](https://committees.parliament.uk/committee/365/business-energy-and-industrial-strategy-committee/) necessitated an update or two, that committee being likely to take an interest in matters pertaining to [proposed negative statutory instruments](https://ukparliament.github.io/ontologies/procedure/maps/legislation/secondary/proposed-negative-sis/proposed-negative-sis.pdf), [legislative reform orders](https://ukparliament.github.io/ontologies/procedure/maps/legislation/secondary/statutory-instruments/super-affirmative-procedures/legislative-reform-orders/legislative-reform-order.pdf) and [treaties](https://ukparliament.github.io/ontologies/procedure/maps/treaties/crag-treaties/crag-treaties.pdf). So that's four maps that needed their pixels and bits tweaked. This week also saw the [European Affairs Committee](https://committees.parliament.uk/committee/516/european-affairs-committee/) hold its [first ever oral evidence session on the matter of a treaty](https://committees.parliament.uk/event/28158/formal-meeting-oral-evidence-session/). In this case the [Agreement, done at Brussels on 14 July 2026, in respect of Gibraltar between the European Union and the European Atomic Energy Community of the one part, and the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland, of the other part](https://api.parliament.uk/procedure-browser/work-packages/llkgYqGm). The choosing of treaty titles being a continuing source of befuddlement to us. To cope with that, our treaty procedure map has gained a [whole new step](https://api.parliament.uk/procedure-browser/steps/rdTJyqtu), the results of which [pop up on that treaty's procedural timeline here](https://api.parliament.uk/procedure-browser/work-packages/llkgYqGm#business-item-bkqLlZ4e-actualising-rdTJyqtu).

At the top end of our procedural value chain, our [Procedure Browsable Space](https://api.parliament.uk/procedure-browser)&trade; continues to get its pixels polished. In recent weeks, work packages gained a new view, that view listing links to [documents considered pertinent to a well-informed debate](https://api.parliament.uk/procedure-browser/work-packages/HbDmR5Lh/documents). The first iteration was all a little hard-coded, any updates to the data also requiring a tweak to the controller code before any changes could be seen on the pages. Our dear reader will no doubt be aware that this is *not our way*. Not our way at all. Not to sound dogmatic, but we would always aim to have as little code between the data and the eyeballs as possible. The only job the code should be doing is grabbing the data and serialising it as appropriate. After all, one would never wish to insert a code monkey between a librarian and her audience. For that reason, Librarian Jayne has churned out [a new SPARQL query](https://github.com/ukparliament/procedure-browser/blob/main/lib/sparql/queries/work_package_documents.rb), the [controller code has been much simplified](https://github.com/ukparliament/procedure-browser/commit/cce8ac81ead46e6d34b2564e79edb14dc72fb8a4), and what appears on the website is now entirely in the hands of Jayne and team. Much better.

Still with Jayne and still with [SPARQL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPARQL), she's also started to refactor the queries used by other pages in our Procedure Browsable Space&trade;. This to add comments, standardise the names of properties returned, apply appropriate indentation, and generally make them more legible to experienced SPARQLheads and newbies alike. For proof of work, please take a look inside the SPARQL queries details element on any of the pages under [calculation styles](https://api.parliament.uk/procedure-browser/calculation-styles). If you do that, you'll also notice each query now links to our [SPARQL endpoint](https://api.parliament.uk/sparql#), so you can tweak the query at will. That not everyone wishes to lift the bonnet should not mean that [nobody can](https://smethur.st/posts/176135871).
