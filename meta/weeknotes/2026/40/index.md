# 2026 - Week 40

## Librarian of the Week

It's been a wee while since we [last handed out our much coveted Librarian of the Week trophy](https://ukparliament.github.io/ontologies/meta/weeknotes/2025/05/#librarian-of-the-week). It's been even longer since a non-librarian got their grubby mitts on it. But this week marks an exception as 'Tech Lead' Andy finds his mantlepiece justly adorned.

And what makes a non-librarian deserving of such a commendation, you may be asking. Well, our Andy has been busy in the background tidying things. If a librarian cannot appreciate effort spent tidying things we may as well all give up and go home.

If you tuned in [last time out](https://ukparliament.github.io/ontologies/meta/weeknotes/2026/38/), you'll already be aware that Andy spent several weeks sweeping up the detritus of our last attempt to establish solid foundations for our Open Data Platform. Thereby saving the body corporate - and indeed the taxpayer - a deal of money. Since then he's popped on his dev-ops hat and started afresh. Now the definition of 'dev-ops' appears to have undergone something of a change since Young Robert and [Michael](https://bsky.app/profile/fantasticlife.bsky.social)'s [salad days](https://allinthehead.com/retro/280/the-future-of-web-apps-summit/). But then a glance through the window reveals a thoroughly modern Westminster, complete with electrical lighting and packed solid with horseless carriages. Even Robert and Michael recognise that times change.

Over the past couple of weeks, Andy has been polishing deployment pipelines and ensuring that all of our infrastructure arrives in the shape of code. Which makes a pleasant change. It also comes at an opportune moment, as the [Parliamentary Computational Section](https://www.parliament.uk/mps-lords-and-offices/offices/bicameral/parliamentary-digital-service/) undertake yet another mission to move assorted boxes in the general direction of the cloud. Andy is currently kept employed with the unenviable task of migrating our [Solr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apache_Solr) search service to a new subscription and the even less enviable task of migrating the piping that sits between our [triplestore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triplestore) and Solr to the same home. All of which will hopefully result in architecture diagrams that look a little less like overcooked linguine.

Andy has also found the time to work with Developer Jon to host Jon's [new, old Parliamentary Search code](https://github.com/ukparliament/search-prototype) over on Azure. Current efforts being deployed and made available to the world by means of [Heroku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heroku). It has to be said that the Azure hosted application feels quite a lot faster than what's currently live. Though Jon suspects that may have something to do with the unimaginable amount of compute required to satisfy the ravenous appetites of the magic sand crawling lads. More testing needed.

Congratulations on your first ever Librarian of the Week trophy, Andy. We're afraid that it comes with no cash bonus and the trophy itself is purely notional. Please don't let it go to your head.

## It's not all pixels you know

Back in the [new, old Parliamentary Search](https://beta-search.parliament.uk/) codebase, Developer Jon remains busy responding to [Librarian Anya](https://bsky.app/profile/anyaso.bsky.social)'s exacting search alias specifications, and Librarian Jayne remains busy testing his efforts. Our dear reader will be pleased to learn that Anya now has a [first pass draft for a page listing all aliases](https://trello.com/c/VOMX01X3/861-draft-alias-document-meta-page) that should appear at some point over the next week or so. Jon may be less pleased.

At this point, our dear reader may well be wondering whether the existence of that page will make search super easy to operate for the novice user. Dear user, it will not. The listing of aliases being of more use to what we like to call our 'power user' - hello Researcher Richard.

Now if there any folks out there from [the cult of the user needs](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/start-by-learning-user-needs), they may well be asking [why don't you concentrate on making it easier for those lacking Richard's considerable brain powers](https://designnotes.blog.gov.uk/2014/05/28/do-the-hard-work-to-make-it-simple/)? That would be because we have what Young Robert might call an Epic on our backlog covering an advanced search form that will also function as a query builder. This enabling search users to click, double click, select, deselect, drag and possibly drop, the results of their efforts resulting in a query being built before their very eyes. And in order to make any of that work, we first need aliases in place. After all, one does not hang the curtains before painting the walls. As it were.

We remain in two minds about what to tackle next. One option being the aforementioned form, the other being data exports. To help inform that decision, Librarian Anya ran [a brief session](https://trello.com/c/CcLxwgaM/892-session-on-advanced-search-csv-exports) in the hope of familiarising those concerned with existing functionality. That looks like a lot of Javascript, said Jon, when confronted with the advanced search form. That looks like a lot of template configuration, he added, when shown the CSV exports. Also, those are not CSVs, he continued. With that, Jon was sent away for the weekend to cogitate. Let's see what mood he's in on Monday.

A handful of other changes to the search application have been more in the way of tidying and bug fixing. To that end, all mentions of the [Parliamentary Office of Science and Technology](https://post.parliament.uk/) as the publisher of a briefing are [always clickable](https://beta-search.parliament.uk/objects?object=http%3A%2F%2Fdata.parliament.uk%2Fresources%2F1763324#secondary-information). Files associated with a deposited paper are [labelled associated files](https://beta-search.parliament.uk/objects?object=http%3A%2F%2Fdata.parliament.uk%2Fdepositedpapers%2F2288149) and not, as before, available files. The option to download a research briefing is [labelled 'Download document'](https://beta-search.parliament.uk/objects?object=http%3A%2F%2Fdata.parliament.uk%2Fresources%2F1195384) and not 'Download full report'. And things lacking titles [no longer shout about it](https://beta-search.parliament.uk/objects?object=http%3A%2F%2Fdata.parliament.uk%2Fpimsdata%2Fhansard%2FPROCEEDING_49738). Much neater.

## Subjects and specialists

With our Library Knowledge Base&trade; rebuilt and out for testing, team:Thesaurus have found themselves on the receiving end of a whole host of feedback from the researchers whose subject specialisms that website attempts to describe. Resulting actions have been broken down into two parts: those requiring adaptations to the application code and those requiring tweaks to the [Parliament Thesaurus](https://lda.data.parliament.uk/terms) that underpins it all.

On the application front, we found ourselves in a happy position whereby all changes were well within the skillset of Young Robert and Michael. A good job really, given [Shedcode](https://shedcode.co.uk/) [James](https://mastodon.me.uk/@jamesjefferies) had taken the week off and appeared to be having a marvellous time [riding upon tiny Welsh trains](https://mastodon.me.uk/@jamesjefferies/117364946078357340). Well, would you like to disturb him? We would not.

Pixels chisels in hand, Young Robert and Michael have prodded the application code meaning the search auto-suggest has gained the display of subject synonyms, and qualifying notes on subject specialisms now appear in the all the places they're meant to appear. Should you be fortunate enough to be in possession of a parliamentary login, why not [take a click](https://library-knowledge-base.parliament.uk/). See what you think.

Back in the Thesaurus Librarians Susannah, Anna and Ned have been merrily rejigging labels, synonyms, scope notes and taxonomic positioning as they respond to feedback on subjects as diverse as [antimicrobial resistance](https://trello.com/c/QIPUlJUx/449-antimicrobial-resistance), [blood](https://trello.com/c/aHeDxmn8/447-blood), [British Overseas Territories](https://trello.com/c/NRhgj7nx/444-british-overseas-territories),
[circular economy](https://trello.com/c/qQ0t7Zsl/458-circular-economy), [the Covid Winter Grant Scheme](https://trello.com/c/fTNsZL7q/441-covid-winter-grant-scheme), [demography and demographics](https://trello.com/c/eUEY5oQx/461-demography-demographics), [Dream Number and the Olympic Lottery](https://trello.com/c/swM3sPRe/451-dream-number-and-olympic-lottery), [drugs both legal and otherwise](https://trello.com/c/2oymQUBC/448-drugs-etc), [housing supply](https://trello.com/c/VCBL1zwJ/460-housing-supply-housebuilding), [the Planning-gain Supplement](https://trello.com/c/BXYIsMZU/429-planning-gain-supplement), [private social renting](https://trello.com/c/Pw3dECZS/459-private-social-renting), [rebates](https://trello.com/c/P2KH1ifq/452-rebates), and [structural adjustments](https://trello.com/c/6CwgHsZN/435-structural-adjustment). ## Introducing parliamentary procedure to hypertext and hoping they get on

A couple of very minor changes to our [Procedure Browsable Space](https://api.parliament.uk/procedure-browser)&trade; are now available for your delectation. 'Powered' as we like to say by Librarian and Backend Developer Jayne's [exquisite SPARQL skills](https://ukparliament.github.io/ontologies/procedure/meta/queries/) and at the suggestion of [Mr Korris](https://bsky.app/profile/mattkorris.bsky.social), we now have [a 'landing page' for secondary legislation](https://api.parliament.uk/procedure-browser/secondary-legislation). And a dear wish that that might come in useful at some point.

Jayne has also been polishing the queries used for our [clock list](https://api.parliament.uk/procedure-browser/clocks#sparql) and [clock page](https://api.parliament.uk/procedure-browser/clocks/Gr3meubW#sparql), so if you have an enduring fascination with procedural time counting and a desire to learn [SPARQL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPARQL), this could be your starting point.

Alerted to a slight mishap by Librarian Jayne, [Librarian Phil](https://bsky.app/profile/philbgorman.bsky.social) has been investigating why the [Members' Names Information System](https://data.parliament.uk/membersdataplatform/#) happens to be in possession of two different versions of the Government Equalities Office. We're told that [the one in use was created in March 2013 and the other one predates any audit trail information being available](https://trello.com/c/oPi2rpbd/544-investigate-when-the-two-geos-were-created-in-mnis-and-search-and-indexing). So that's less than ideal.

## A Member mix up

Off the back of our [Maiden Speech publication](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/sn04588/), a [letter of correction flooded in](https://trello.com/c/d0A8dq65/174-update-to-maiden-speeches-pff-incorrect-mp-name). Please could you update the name of James Harden to Richard Harden we were asked. That's strange, we thought. I wonder how we managed to get that wrong? Checking and re-checking assorted sources, it turned out our correspondent was quite correct. At least if obituaries in The Times and [The Telegraph](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/news/obituaries/1371935/Major-Richard-Harden.html) are to be believed. Not to mention [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Harden_(politician)). In fairness, his given name was James so it's a fair mistake for someone to have made.

Confused nonetheless, [Librarian Emily](https://bsky.app/profile/emilyjdavi.bsky.social) popped on her deerstalker, drew deep on her pipe, and took out her magnifying glass. We now think we've got to the bottom of matters, Richard first appearing as James in the [Rush database](https://membersafter1832.historyofparliamentonline.org/). From where we think it was reused by [Historic Hansard](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/index.html), then by [They Work for You](https://www.theyworkforyou.com/mp/17672/james_harden/county_armagh), then by our own publications. Emily has corrected across [Rush](https://membersafter1832.historyofparliamentonline.org/members/4252) and across our own [Parliamentary Facts and Figures](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/tag/parliament-facts-and-figures/) publications. Editing Historic Hansard and TWFY being somewhat outside our capabilities.

## Dusting the corners

If you've been following along from the comfort of your sofa, you'll be well aware that a tag team of crack librarians have been busy linking Acts of Parliament to their preceding bills in the state they were in when they left Parliament. And linking those instantiations to their preceding version and etc. As if the British state were quite unaware of [FRBR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_Requirements_for_Bibliographic_Records). This week, Librarian Emma was back on duty, [adding links to all Acts given Royal Assent between 1993 and 1995](https://trello.com/c/A0BoL4eR/455-acts-without-previous-version-link-1993-1995).

Librarian Martin has also been busy, [linking all statutory instruments to any ensuing debate](https://trello.com/c/rSdbFaTm/452-adding-debates-as-related-items-to-sis-1998-2006), this work covering off 1998 to 2006.

And finally, [Librarian Ayesha](https://bsky.app/profile/askalibrarylady.bsky.social) has lent her hand to the tidying of 10 written statements, all of which had somehow found themselves untouched by caring librarian hands. One of those turned out to be a duplicate, which has now been deleted. The rest have all been [indexed as appropriate](https://trello.com/c/qo8Dx0jW/471-unindexed-written-statements).
